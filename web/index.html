Formulas you can watch, drag and take apart.

A CAD-style workbench with 39 live formulas, from polar coordinates to the Lorenz attractor, sorting, pathfinding and proofs.

Each formula is a resizable viewport on an infinite drafting canvas. It comes with its reference docs, step-by-step derivations, and working Python, TypeScript and Rust code.

Open the workbench Source on GitHub

Runs in your browser with WebAssembly. Best on a desktop with a mouse. Or download the desktop app for macOS, Windows or Linux.

What you can do

Everything happens on one board, the way you'd lay out a drawing.

Place formulas on an infinite canvas

Click a tool to place it, or drag out its size. Move, resize, duplicate, lock, hide and undo, with snapping and smart guides. Every viewport keeps animating at any zoom.

Get your hands on it

Drag points, paint cells, draw walls, orbit 3D views, and scrub through every step of an algorithm like a video.

See it explained

The Inspector shows each formula's equations, controls and full docs. The Derive tab works the equations out with the viewport's own numbers, one operation at a time.

Take the code with you

Export Python (numpy and matplotlib), TypeScript and Rust programs, LaTeX with the current values, or a ready-to-run Jupyter notebook.

Save the whole board

A sheet is a plain-text .fwb file with every formula's settings. Open one later and pick up where you left off.

Link to a formula

Every tool has its own address, like /app/#mandelbrot, so you can share exactly what you're looking at.

Coordinate Systems & Projections 2

POL

Polar to Cartesian

x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ

Drag the point, or switch to the rose curve r = cos kθ and watch it draw petals.
PRJ

Perspective projection

x' = f·x/z + cx

Drag to orbit a 3D scatter plot; the inset shows one ray from above. Try the dolly zoom.

Curves & Interpolation 5

LRP

Linear interpolation

v(t) = v0 + t(v1 − v0)

Drag the endpoints; blend positions, plain numbers and colors.
BEZ

Cubic Bézier curve

B(t) = (1−t)³P0 + 3(1−t)²t P1 + 3(1−t)t² P2 + t³P3

Drag the control points; see de Casteljau's lerps of lerps and the Bernstein weights.
FOU

Drawn by circles

z(t) = Σ c_k e^(ikt)

Pick one of 64 animals; slide the number of circles down and watch the details melt away.
LIS

Lissajous figures

x = A sin(at + δπ), y = B sin(bt)

Set amplitudes, frequencies and phase; δ drifts so the figure morphs and seems to turn in 3D.
EXP

Expression plotter

y = a sin(bx) · r = 1 + cos(3θ) · (cos t, sin t)

Type up to six rows; every free letter gets a slider. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom; the code export contains your expressions.

Physics & Network Layouts 1

FRC

Springs and repulsion

Fs = −k(d − x0), Fr = kr/d²

Drag a node and watch the graph rebalance; hover a node for its force arrows.

Data Scaling & Normalization 1

NRM

Min-max normalization

x' = (x − min) / (max − min)

Push the outlier up and watch every other bar get squashed; hover a bar for its calculation.

Fractals & Generative Patterns 3

MAN

Mandelbrot set

z(n+1) = z(n)² + c

Click to zoom in, right-click to zoom out, drag to pan; hover to see a point's orbit.
FLW

Perlin noise flow field

θ = N(x, y, t) · 2π · k

Particles with fading trails follow the noise; hover to read the field, or show the vectors.
LIF

Conway's Game of Life

born with 3 neighbours, survives with 2 or 3

Draw and erase cells, load classic patterns, hover a cell to see the rule at work.

Chaos & Dynamical Systems 1

LOR

Lorenz attractor

dx/dt = σ(y − x), dy/dt = x(ρ − z) − y, dz/dt = xy − βz

Drag to orbit; tune σ, ρ, β; follow up to three nearly identical starts as they part.

Geometry & Spatial Analysis 3

DST

Euclidean distance

d = √((x2 − x1)² + (y2 − y1)²)

Move the mouse to find the nearest point, or switch to K-means and watch clusters form.
ROT

2D rotation

x' = x cos θ − y sin θ, y' = x sin θ + y cos θ

Drag the point; tilt the crowded month labels until they stop colliding.
VOR

Voronoi diagram

V(pᵢ) = {x : d(x, pᵢ) ≤ d(x, pⱼ) for all j ≠ i}

Drag, add and remove seeds; Lloyd relaxation, Delaunay edges, giraffe, cracked-mud and dragonfly-wing styles.

Linear Algebra 1

MAT

Matrix transformation

x' = a x + b y, y' = c x + d y

Drag the basis tips and the grid warps; det shading, eigenvectors, presets, and a chain of up to three matrices.

Statistics & Trend Analysis 1

REG

Line of best fit

minimize Σ (yᵢ − (m xᵢ + b))²

Drag points, click to add, right-click to remove; residuals, squared errors and R².

Animation & Easing 1

EAS

Cubic ease-out

f(t) = 1 − (1 − t)³

The curve and its speed, a linear vs eased race, and bars gliding into place.

Color & Accessibility 1

LUM

Relative luminance

Y = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B

The heatmap picks black or white text for each cell; contrast ratios; WCAG linearization.

Sorting 9

BUB

Bubble sort

O(n²), O(n) best

Neighbours swap until each pass carries the largest value to the end.
SEL

Selection sort

O(n²) always, at most n − 1 swaps

The smallest unsorted value (purple) is swapped to the front.
INS

Insertion sort

O(n²), O(n) best

Each key slides left into place; try the Nearly sorted preset.
MRG

Merge sort

O(n log n), O(n) space

Split into halves, then merge them back; brackets show the recursion.
QCK

Quick sort

O(n log n) average, O(n²) worst

Partition around the pivot (pink), then sort each side.
Q3W

Quick sort (3-way)

O(n log n), fast with repeated keys

Keys equal to the pivot gather in the middle; try Few unique.
HEP

Heap sort

O(n log n), O(1) space

The list drawn as a heap: build it, then pull out the largest each time.
CNT

Counting sort

O(n + k)

Tally every value, then write the output straight from the counts.
RCE

Sorting race

same list, same steps per second

Race the algorithms you pick, with live comparisons, moves and a measured time.

Graphs & Trees 7

BFS

Breadth-first search

dist(n) = dist(u) + 1

Explores in rings of equal distance, so its first path is the shortest in steps.
DFS

Depth-first search

newest frontier cell first (a stack)

Dives down one corridor before backing up; finds a path, rarely the shortest.
DIJ

Dijkstra's algorithm

dist(n) = min(dist(n), dist(u) + w(n))

Settles cells by total cost, so it walks around mud when a detour is cheaper.
AST

A* search

f(n) = g(n) + h(n)

Dijkstra steered by a straight-line guess of the distance left; visits fewer cells.
BST

Binary search tree

AVL: balance factor within 1

Insert, delete and find keys; toggle AVL; in-, pre- and post-order walks; rotations glide into place.
KRU

Kruskal's algorithm

minimize Σ w(e) over the n − 1 tree edges

Takes edges by weight, keeping each one that joins two trees; drag the points.
PRI

Prim's algorithm

minimize Σ w(e) over the n − 1 tree edges

Grows one tree from a vertex, always by the lightest edge leaving it.

Proofs & Derivations 3

QDR

Quadratic formula

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

Slide a, b and c; roots, vertex and axis; the Derive tab completes the square.
PYT

Pythagorean theorem

a² + b² = c²

Four triangles slide around an (a + b)² square, or the altitude splits it into two copies.
EUL

Euler's formula

e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ

The Taylor series as arrows spiralling onto the unit circle; θ = π gives e^(iπ) + 1 = 0.
